# DarkFrame Map Seeding and Economy Analysis

## Terrain Distribution  
- **Wasteland (~40%)** – This leaves ~60% of tiles with resources or structures. As one design guideline notes, if resource tiles are *too sparse*, early game becomes frustrating, whereas *too many* makes expansion trivial【8†L212-L219】. At ~60% resource coverage, early frustration is unlikely severe, but reducing wasteland slightly (raising resource density) could ease early expansion. Fine-tuning may depend on player data.  
- **Metal vs Energy (20% each)** – In Total Annihilation (a classic RTS with metal/energy), designers intentionally made metal much scarcer to force strategic trade-offs: “metal is usually rather scarce in the early parts of a mission” whereas energy is easier to obtain【18†L130-L139】. If DarkFrame’s T2+ units consume more metal, a slightly lower metal density than energy could add strategic tension. However, symmetric 20/20% is simpler; any change should be balanced against player spending (e.g. if combat reliance on metal is very high).  
- **Caves (8%, 1,800 tiles)** – At a 2.5% base drop rate and 20% chance each drop is a digger, each full sweep yields ~33 diggers. This pace seems moderate: players get a steady trickle of diggers without a flood. Modern games often use “pity” or guaranteed-drop systems to prevent dry spells (e.g. Genshin Impact guarantees a rare pull after many failures【21†L149-L153】). Here, giving one guaranteed digger per 75 caves is akin to a pity timer and should feel fair. The exponential digger bonus caps at +200%, which (with ~33 diggers) yields only ~+46% bonus – far from oppressive. Using an exponential decay formula (bonus = 200×(1–e^(–0.008n))) ensures each new digger adds benefit but with strong diminishing returns【23†L75-L83】. In short, the digger yield and bonus appear reasonable.  
- **Forests (2%)** – Forests use the same drop logic as caves but are marketed as “better loot.” If forests are truly meant to be special, they should have a distinct drop table or buff (e.g. higher digger rate or unique items) to justify their rarity. Otherwise they function identically to caves, which may confuse players. Many games make rarer terrain types yield unique resources or bonuses. Keeping forest at 2% retains its scarcity; if feedback shows forests feel redundant, consider enhancing their drops.  
- **Factories (10%, 2,250 tiles)** – This creates numerous potential PvP hotspots. The sufficiency of 2,250 depends on peak concurrent players: roughly one factory per ~10 players yields some contention but not constant bottlenecks. Many strategy/MMO designs cluster strategic points to promote fights. If overcrowding occurs, factory count could be tweaked or phased (e.g. only some spawn resources). For now, 10% is likely adequate to ensure contested zones without saturating.  
- **Banks (4 tiles)** – Only four banks (one in each quadrant) means some players must travel far to deposit resources. Games often balance convenience vs. strategic travel; 4 banks keeps them rare strategic targets, but if players complain about travel time, adding a few (e.g. 8–12 in a diamond grid) could help. More banks would dilute value, though. Empirical data (player travel times) should guide whether to add bank locations.  
- **Shrine (1 tile)** – At (1,1) in a corner, the Shrine buffs mining for whoever reaches it. A more **central location** would likely make it a highly contested objective. Many games place powerful buff sites centrally to force conflict. Moving the Shrine toward map center (or at least away from map edge) would encourage mid-game fights. (No source cited; this follows standard map design.)  

## Harvest and Bonuses  
- **Base harvest (400–750)** – This uniform range yields an average 575 resources per tile (before bonuses). Whether this supports the intended economy curve depends on build costs and sink rates. Generally, mid-game should see production rising to meet escalating costs. We lack exact spending data, but the range seems reasonable: it’s neither trivially small nor excessively large. As one design rule-of-thumb notes, resource income must match spending needs or players will stagnate. Tuning may require monitoring how quickly the average player accumulates resources vs. how fast they spend them (e.g. on units or upgrades).  
- **Max single-tile harvest (~5,681)** – With all bonuses (digger bonus +200%, shrine, VIP, flag), the theoretical ceiling is ~5.68k per tile. In practice, most players will see much less. This cap feels acceptable: it allows a “rich” harvest but still requires significant investment in buffs. Many games use diminishing return formulas (exponential decay) so that caps are unreachable by normal means【23†L75-L83】. Here the cap mainly serves to bound the extreme cases.  
- **Digger cap (200%)** – Because real gameplay yields ~33 diggers and that yields only ~+46% bonus, the 200% cap is not practically hit. This means players benefit from each digger without ever feeling “capped,” which is good design. Using an exponential formula provides smooth diminishing returns【23†L75-L83】. The cap exists mostly as a mathematical limit and won’t feel oppressive.  
- **Guaranteed drop (1 per 75 caves)** – This “pity” mechanic ensures players can’t go too long without a digger. Many modern games adopt such systems to keep players engaged (e.g. Overwatch loot boxes, Genshin gacha)【21†L149-L153】. A 75-cave guarantee (roughly 1 every half-day of cave activity) is a fair pace, preventing frustration while still making diggers somewhat rare.  

## Progression  
- **XP Curve (250×L^2.5)** – This is a steep, super-linear curve: Level 50 requires ~4.4M XP. As with any exponential-style curve, leveling slows dramatically at higher levels【30†L113-L120】. The small XP from harvesting (3 XP/tile) means solo gatherers level very slowly (as shown in the table). Combat and objectives yield much more XP (300 per win, 200 per factory), so active players will advance far faster. This disparity appears **intentional**: many games give far more XP for fighting/challenges than passive tasks. The result should be balanced: casual grinders will level at a reasonable pace, while fighters get swift progress. The exponential nature of the curve【30†L113-L120】 means high levels are hard to reach without substantial combat/quest XP.  
- **Research Points (RP)** – At 1 RP per level, players get ~30 RP by level 30, which is far below the T4 cost (350 RP). The design clearly expects *other sources* of RP (e.g. daily harvest milestones, achievements, events). Many games supplement level-based currency with daily/log-in bonuses and achievements to bridge such gaps. If players find progression too slow, consider adding more accessible RP (daily goals or small quests). In short, it seems the curve is deliberately steep, making final tiers an **aspirational goal** rather than a routine unlock. Many MMOs intentionally set late-game goals very high to keep veteran players engaged, so having T5 at L50 (practically unattainable for most) can serve as a long-term target.  

## Strategic Considerations

One suggested solution is “spawn randomly on the circumference of the circle” so players start around the map edge【15†L588-L592】. This yields two benefits: (a) **fair spacing** – new players are guaranteed a minimum distance apart, reducing early griefing; (b) **nearby resources** – map edges can be seeded with starter resources. The diagram above (from developer discussion) illustrates how outer-ring spawning avoids central clustering of spawns【15†L588-L592】.*  
- **Shrine location** – (Related to above) Placing the Shrine in a corner means it’s rarely contested after early game. A central Shrine would create a natural PvP focal point. (No direct citation; this follows typical map design wisdom.)  
- **Bank arrangement** – The four existing banks form an X (corners and center). A “diamond” pattern at 1/3 and 2/3 positions might be more symmetrical. For example, placing banks at (25,25), (125,25), (75,75), (25,125), (125,125) could ease travel distances. More banks (8–12 total) would improve convenience but reduce strategic value of each. This is a design choice balancing convenience vs. contest.  
- **Resource clustering vs uniform** – Currently terrain is shuffled uniformly, meaning resources are scattered. Many modern strategy games intentionally cluster resources for thematic “biomes” or hotspots【2†L125-L132】. Clustering (e.g. by cellular automata) produces natural-looking resource patches instead of pure randomness【2†L125-L132】. Clustering can create distinct regions (rich vs. poor), which may add strategic variety. If playtesting shows too much wandering for resources, introducing moderate clustering could help. Otherwise, uniform distribution is simpler.  
- **Spawn bias** – As noted, spawning new players on the outer ring can increase fairness and give them guaranteed nearby resources【15†L588-L592】. This contrasts with random spawns (which can cluster or leave some new players isolated). A ring or multi-ring spawn strategy (e.g. outer 20% of map) would ensure new arrivals start roughly equidistant, with ample nearby harvest tiles to begin safely. The cited game-dev discussion explicitly recommends circumference spawning to make new spawns fair【15†L588-L592】.  
- **Auction House vs Shrine proximity** – The AH at (10,10) is very close to the Shrine at (1,1). If this causes congestion or unintended synergies, moving one of them could be wise. Generally, key POIs (trading post vs buff shrine) might be separated so players traverse the map. We suggest considering moving the Auction House (or Shrine) farther apart to spread activity.
